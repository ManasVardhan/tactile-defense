# Unit Squads, Hex Deployment and Level Ladder: Design

Date: 2026-11-24. Status: awaiting approval.

Lines marked **(decided)** were chosen by the project owner in conversation. Lines marked **(call)** were chosen by
the designer and are open to correction. Everything else is left to the builder.

## 1. Intent and why

Today the player commands one blob of about 40 soldiers (identical apart from one leader and an optional
mercenary), moved by tapping and shaped by drawing a line. The owner
wants the battle to be about choosing and positioning different kinds of troops instead:

1. Build a squad out of distinct unit types (tank, attacker, healer, sniper, vanguard, demolisher), each with its own
   always-on ability.
2. Deploy and command those units by clicking: click a unit button, click a hexagon to deploy it; click a deployed
   unit, click another hexagon to move it. Drawing formations is removed.
3. Unlock unit types by passing the game's levels.
4. Levels 1 to 6 are designed levels of rising difficulty, with map layouts changed as needed. Level 1 is the
   tutorial, and the tutorial explains everything a brand new player needs. (added by the owner after approval)

The owner did not state a deeper reason beyond wanting these mechanics; treat the three points above as the
requirement. From this point the game is its own design. New mechanics, names, UI and art in this work are original
and are not modeled on any commercial title.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- In an attack battle the player deploys units of 3 soldiers onto hexes and moves each unit independently.
- Six unit types that play visibly differently.
- A 10-level ladder that gates unit types and squad size.
- A squad builder screen where the player picks which unlocked units to bring.
- All ten levels are winnable and rise in difficulty with the new army.
- The project stays green: existing tests that still apply keep passing, new behavior has tests.
- A playable web build at each checkpoint (section 11).

**Non-goals (this round)**
- Triggered or cooldown abilities. All abilities are passive.
- Hand-designing Levels 7 to 10. They reuse the existing bases `b07` to `b10`.
- Changing defense battles (the AI raiding the player's base) or the base editor.
- Enemy armies built from the new unit types.
- Mobile (Android/iOS) verification, multiplayer, renaming the project.
- Final unit art. First pass needs units to be tellable apart, not polished.

**Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)**
- Unit types that feel the same in play.
- Clicks that land on the wrong hex or the wrong unit, or a grid the player cannot see when they need it.
- Breaking defense battles or the menu flow while rebuilding attack battles.

## 3. Current state (what the builder inherits)

- `Squad/SquadController` is a singleton that owns every player soldier, the formation slots, the ground ring and
  obstacle routing. It spawns 40 soldiers plus up to 20 from research; one is a leader (Hero prefab, `leaderConfig`),
  and a hired mercenary is added on top.
- Capture today has no minimum: any one uncontested player soldier on the pad progresses it, with a speed bonus of
  3 percent per soldier capped at 10 soldiers (`Battle/CapturePoint`, `BattleConfig`).
- Walls and fences are static props without `Health`, baked into `NavGrid` as permanent obstacles. Mines and geysers
  are hidden structures on the lane. Structures do not share one targeting path: the sniper tower weights its
  targets, the tesla chains from the nearest, mines, geysers and lobbed shells act by proximity or area.
- There is no web build script in `Assets/Scripts/Editor`. `TactileWarsWeb/` was built by hand on Nov 17 with gzip
  compression. `CameraRig/BattleCamera` follows its centroid. `BattleManager` reads it for the lose check and
  reinforcements. `UI/Battle/BattleHUD` shows its soldier count.
- `Formation/*` turns a drawn stroke into soldier slots. `Battle/AttackerAI` (defense battles only) drives the same
  `SquadController` through `SetFormation` and `MoveFormationTo`.
- `Units/Soldier` holds health, motor, targeting and weapon, and is ticked by whatever owns it. `Squad/SoldierMotor`
  steers toward a target point with separation and obstacle sliding. `Squad/NavGrid` is a 1-unit obstacle grid with
  A* and line of sight.
- Targeting is nearest-in-range (`Combat/Targeting`, `UnitRegistry.FindNearest`).
- There is no gameplay grid. The hexagons on the ground are a texture.
- There are no levels. `Meta` has an XP level (1 to 20) and ten AI bases (`b01` to `b10`, ordered by difficulty in
  `Editor/ProgressionBuilder`) that can be raided in any order subject to `BaseDefinition.requiredLevel`.
- `UI/Menu/AttackScreen` lists the bases. `UI/Menu/ArmyScreen` shows squad stats and mercenaries.
- Data assets and prefabs are generated by editor builders (`AssetBuilder`, `ProgressionBuilder`, `SceneBuilder`),
  not hand-authored. New data should follow that pattern.
- Tests: EditMode 42, PlayMode 30, including a bot that auto-plays bases for balance (`BotPlaythroughTests`).

## 4. Approaches considered

1. **New unit system for the player; legacy squad kept only for the AI attacker. (chosen, call)** A hex grid, a
   3-soldier unit and a player army controller replace `SquadController` in attack battles, reusing `Soldier`,
   `SoldierMotor`, `NavGrid`, `Weapon` and `Health`. Player stroke input is deleted. Defense battles keep the legacy
   squad and `AttackerAI` untouched. Cost: two army implementations coexist. Benefit: defense battles and their
   tests are not at risk.
2. **Many small `SquadController`s.** Fast start, but the class is a singleton built around formations and rings;
   hex occupancy would be bolted on. Rejected.
3. **Convert everything including the AI attacker.** Cleanest end state, about double the work, rewrites a mode
   outside the request. Deferred.

The owner left the build approach to the designer.

## 5. Battle design

### 5.1 Hex grid (hidden)
- A real hex grid covers the playable map and is still the rule: one player unit per hex, snapping, blocking and
  the capture pad rule all work on it. **(decided: hex deployment; call: one per hex)**
- **The player never sees it.** The battlefield reads as an open field: no hex tiles or seams on the ground, no hex
  outlines or fills, no hex paving props. Each hex is a "spot" to the player. **(decided after the owner played
  the build: the visible hexes looked shoddy; the grid stays underneath)**
- A hex must hold 3 soldiers standing in a small triangle without overlapping. Outer radius 1.5 world units
  (raised from 1.4 when soldiers became 20 percent bigger), soldier slots at half the outer radius from the
  center, pointy top.
- A hex is **blocked** if an obstacle or structure overlaps it (derive from `NavGrid` and structure colliders), and
  **occupied** if a player unit stands on it or is walking to it.
- While a deploy button is armed or a unit is selected, a round hover marker about one unit wide sits on the spot
  under the pointer: a soft white-gold ring where a click would be obeyed, a red ring with a cross where it would be
  refused (blocked, taken, an enemy standing there, off the field). It snaps from spot to spot as the pointer
  moves, which is how the player learns that positions are discrete without seeing a grid. Nothing is drawn over a
  friendly unit (a click there selects it). The markers are hidden otherwise. **(call)**

### 5.2 Deploying
- The battle is real time with automatic firing, as today. **(call)**
- The bottom of the HUD has one button per unit type in the squad, with an icon and a remaining count
  (for example "Attacker x2"). **(decided: unit buttons)**
- Click a button to arm it, then click a spot on the field: that unit's 3 soldiers appear on the hex under it.
  **(decided)**
- Deployment is allowed on any hex of the map, at any time during the battle, except blocked or occupied hexes and
  hexes with an enemy soldier standing in them. **(decided: anywhere, any time; call: the enemy-soldier exclusion)**
- Hidden traps (mines, unrevealed geysers) do not block a hex and are never revealed by the overlay. A unit deployed
  or moved onto one sets it off as usual. **(call)**
- Each squad entry can be deployed once. After a deploy the button disarms; clicking the armed button again or
  pressing Escape cancels. **(call)**

### 5.3 Selecting and moving
- Click a deployed unit (any of its soldiers or its hex) to select it. The unit shows a selection marker.
  **(decided)**
- With a unit selected, click a free spot: the unit walks there along a `NavGrid` path and takes up the triangle on
  arrival. It keeps firing at anything in range while moving. The destination is reserved when the order is given
  and the origin is freed when the unit leaves. **(decided: click to move; call: reservation)**
- Clicking a friendly unit always selects it: clicking another one switches selection, and clicking the selected
  unit again keeps it selected (players re-click out of habit before the next order; a silent deselect made that
  order do nothing). Only Escape deselects, and arming a unit bar button clears the selection. The unit stays
  selected after a move order so it can be redirected. **(call; changed after the first browser playthrough)**
- A click on a blocked or occupied hex is rejected: the red crossed ring flashes on that spot for 0.3 s with the
  reject sound. The hover marker already shows red before the click. While a button is armed or a unit is selected
  the active capture circle's own ring glows gold ("stand here"); the counting rule is unchanged (a unit counts when
  its hex is a pad hex or a soldier stands inside the drawn ring). The selection ring stays as it was. **(call;
  changed with the hidden grid: no hex fills or pad tint)**
- Click priority, highest first: UI, enemy flag bubble (existing tap-to-pop), armed deploy, friendly unit, move
  order. **(call)**
- Touch behaves the same as mouse (tap = click). A click counts however short it is, including a press and release
  that land in the same frame.

### 5.4 Camera
The camera can no longer follow one squad. Dragging on the map pans it, clamped to map bounds. It starts framed on
the player's edge of the map. Camera shake stays. **(call)**

### 5.5 Unit types
Each type is one data asset (stats plus trait flags), following the `StructureConfig` pattern. Numbers are starting
points relative to the Attacker and are to be tuned with the playthrough bot.

| Type | Role | Stats vs Attacker | Always-on ability | Unlock |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attacker | All-rounder | baseline | none | start |
| Tank | Front line | health x3, range x0.6, speed x0.7 | **Draws fire:** enemy soldiers and structures that have a Tank soldier in range target it before nearer targets | start |
| Healer | Support | health x0.8, no weapon | **Mend:** restores health over time to injured friendly soldiers on its own and neighboring hexes. **Revive:** every several seconds brings back one dead soldier in a neighboring unit that still has a soldier alive | clear Level 1 |
| Sniper | Long range | range x2, damage per shot x3, fire interval x3, health x0.6 | long range is the ability | clear Level 2 |
| Vanguard | Fast flanker | speed x2, damage x0.6 | **Seize:** counts double toward capturing a point | clear Level 3 |
| Demolisher | Siege | speed x0.8, slow lobbed shot with splash | **Wrecker:** x3 damage to enemy defenses (turrets, bunkers, cannons and other structures with health), x0.5 to soldiers; prefers structures as targets | clear Level 4 |

All abilities are passive. **(decided)** The roster and unlock levels were proposed by the designer and accepted
without changes. A unit whose three soldiers are all dead is destroyed and cannot be revived by a Healer.

- **Walls stay indestructible. (call)** The roster shown to the owner said the Demolisher wrecks walls; that part is
  dropped for this round because walls have no health and are baked into pathfinding, and breakable walls would let
  gate-based maps be bypassed. The Demolisher's bonus applies to defenses only.
- **Draws fire scope. (call)** Tank priority applies to direct-fire weapons that pick a target: enemy soldiers,
  turrets, bunkers, the tank structure and the sniper tower (a Tank in range outranks the tower's own weighting). It
  does not affect the tesla chain, mines, geysers or lobbed area shots.
- **No leader. (call)** The player army has no leader soldier any more.

Units must be tellable apart at a glance: a type icon above the unit and on its button, plus a visible difference in
the soldiers themselves (scale, accent color, or a simple generated prop). **(call)** Polished per-type art is out of
scope; the owner likes to pick visual designs from several rendered options, so offer options at a checkpoint rather
than deciding alone.

Because the Healer makes health matter, each unit shows three small health pips when damaged or selected. **(call)**

### 5.6 Squad size and army strength
- The squad holds 3 units at the start, plus one more after clearing each of Levels 1 to 5, so 8 units
  (24 soldiers) from Level 6 on. Duplicates are allowed. **(call; revised from the 4-to-8 schedule shown to the
  owner so that it fits six designed levels)**
- This is fewer soldiers than today's 40, so per-soldier strength and every base's enemy multipliers need retuning.

### 5.7 Battle rules that change
- **Capture:** unchanged in principle: any one uncontested player soldier progresses the capture. Each Vanguard
  soldier counts as two toward the speed bonus. The per-soldier bonus and its cap are retuned for armies of at most
  24 soldiers. **(call)**
- **Defeat:** the player loses when no player soldier is alive on the field, nothing is left to deploy, and the
  reinforcement meter is not ready. If the meter is ready the battle continues so the player can use it. Holding
  undeployed units is never a loss. **(call)**
- **Reinforcement meter:** stays, fills as today. When full, pressing its button makes the most recently destroyed
  squad unit deployable again (its button relights). It no longer spawns loose soldiers. **(call)**
- **Mercenary:** a hired mercenary appears as one extra deploy button, a single strong soldier on its own hex,
  outside the squad slots. It counts as a unit for the defeat check, can be mended by a Healer, cannot be revived,
  and cannot be brought back by the meter. **(call)**
- **Research:** multipliers (health, fire rate, damage, move speed, reinforcement charge) apply to every unit type.
  The "Squad Size" and "Bigger Backup" nodes no longer mean anything and are removed from the tree; gold already
  spent on them in an existing save is refunded on load. "Leader Training" (`HeroAbility`) now buffs only the
  mercenary and is relabeled to say so. **(call)**
- **Removed from attack battles:** drawing strokes, the stroke renderer, the squad ground ring, tap-to-move-the-blob.
  Delete what nothing uses afterward; keep whatever the AI attacker still needs.
- **Tutorial:** the overlay is rewritten for the new controls: arm a button, deploy, select, move.
- **Defense battles:** unchanged.

## 6. Level ladder
- There are ten levels. Levels 1 to 6 are hand-designed (section 6.1) and replace bases `b01` to `b06`; Levels 7
  to 10 are the existing bases `b07` to `b10`. **(decided: ladder; decided: Levels 1 to 6 designed, Level 1 is the
  tutorial; call: 7 to 10 kept as they are)**
- A level is playable when the previous one has been cleared. Cleared levels can be replayed. The old XP-level lock
  on bases is removed; XP, gold and research otherwise work as today. **(call)**
- The Attack screen becomes the ladder: each entry shows its number, name, state (locked, open, cleared) and, where
  one exists, the reward for clearing it ("Unlocks Healer", "+1 squad slot"). **(call)**
- On first clearing a level that grants something, the result screen announces it. **(call)**
- The save stores the highest level cleared; unit availability and slot count are derived from it. Existing saves
  load with nothing cleared. **(call)**
- Built so a ladder entry can later point at a hand-designed map without rework. **(decided)**

### 6.1 Designed levels **(call, except where marked)**
Each level is built around the unit the player has just unlocked, so the unit is the obvious answer to the level's
problem. Layouts may change freely **(decided)**. Enemy strength rises from level to level.

| Level | Player has | Teaches | Layout idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Tutorial | Attacker, Tank (3 slots) | Deploy, select, move, pan, front line, capture, win and lose | Short straight lane. A tiny squad at point 1, one turret and a squad with a flag bearer at point 2. No hidden traps, no counterattack |
| 2 | + Healer (4 slots) | Keeping units alive through a long fight | Longer lane with several squads in a row and no place to rest; attrition punishes an army without healing |
| 3 | + Sniper (5 slots) | Range | Open ground crossed by turrets and a bunker that outrange Attackers; raised or walled firing spots that reward shooting from far away |
| 4 | + Vanguard (6 slots) | Speed and flanking | Wide map with two routes and a counterattack on the first point; a fast unit can take the far point or get back in time |
| 5 | + Demolisher (7 slots) | Breaking defenses | A fortified approach: clustered bunkers, cannons and turrets that soldiers alone cannot grind through |
| 6 | all six (8 slots) | Combining everything | A full base mixing every earlier problem, including hidden mines and geysers |

Level 1 must not punish a first player: its enemies deal 0.9 times normal damage, and its result grade ignores the
time component (the time circle shows a dash), since a player reading tutorial cards is slow by design.

Each of Levels 2 to 5 opens with a one-time card introducing the new unit: what it does and one line on how to use
it. Levels 7 to 10 get no new design.

### 6.2 Tutorial (Level 1) **(decided: Level 1 is the tutorial and covers what a new player needs; call: the steps)**
Written for someone who has never played a strategy game. Short sentences, one idea at a time, each step shown next
to the thing it talks about and completed by doing it, not by reading. Enemies on Level 1 stay passive until the
player's units come into range, so nobody is punished for reading.

Guided steps, in order:
1. "This is your squad. Click the Attacker." (points at the unit bar)
2. "Now click a glowing spot to drop them in." (a few suggested spots glow as pulsing gold discs; any valid spot
   works)
3. "A unit is 3 soldiers. They shoot on their own when an enemy is close."
4. "Click your unit, then click where it should go." (a glowing disc suggests a spot up the lane)
5. "Drag the ground to look around."
6. "Now move your units up the lane toward the enemy." (one suggested spot about 5 spots up the lane from the
   foremost unit; done when a unit has moved 4 spots up the lane or when step 7's trigger fires)
7. "Enemies ahead. Tanks are tough and enemies shoot them first. Put your Tank in front." (when the first enemies
   come into view; points at the Tank button, and while the Tank is not deployed 2 free spots in front of the
   foremost unit, toward the nearest enemy, glow; with the Tank already out the card is information only)
8. "Stand on the circle to capture it." (when the first point is clear of enemies, also while step 7's card is up,
   which then ends at once; a spot on the pad glows)

Cards keep clear of what they point at, of the units and of the suggested spots: each takes the side with the most
free screen space.

One-time hints, shown the first time the situation happens:
- A soldier dies: "The dots over a unit show how many soldiers it has left."
- A flag bubble appears: "Pop the bubble to knock out the whole enemy squad."
- A click is rejected: "You can't go there. Pick an open spot."
- The backup meter fills: "Backup is ready. Click it to get a lost unit back."
- First point captured: "Capture both circles to win. You lose if all your units are gone."

No player-facing text says "hex" or "hexagon" (checked by `PlayerTextTests`). Each step and hint is remembered in
the save and never repeats. A "Skip tutorial" control is always visible. The
tutorial runs only on Level 1.

## 7. Squad builder
- Lives on the Army screen, above the mercenary roster. **(call)**
- Shows the squad slots (4 to 8) and a palette of unit types. Locked types are visible but greyed, with the level
  that unlocks them. Click a type to add it to the next empty slot; click a filled slot to clear it. **(call)**
- The squad is saved in the profile. A new or migrated profile gets a sensible default (Attackers and one Tank).
  When a slot is gained it starts filled with an Attacker so the player is never short. **(call)**
- A battle cannot start with an empty squad.

## 8. Constraints
- Unity 6000.3.22f1, URP, the Input System through `Core/PointerInput` (mouse and touch share one path, simulated in
  tests).
- Web is the playtest target. The WebGL build must stay loadable and performant; fewer soldiers than today should
  make this easier.
- Data and prefabs are produced by the editor builders so the project can be regenerated.
- No em dashes in docs, UI text or commit messages. No AI attribution in commits.

## 9. Left to the builder
- Hex size, orientation, coordinate scheme and how blocked hexes are computed.
- Class layout, as long as attack battles do not depend on `SquadController` and defense battles still work.
- Exact stats, heal and revive rates, and per-level enemy tuning, found with the bot.
- The look of the grid overlay, selection marker, pips and unit differentiation (subject to the options note in 5.5).
- Which formation files survive, based on what the AI attacker still uses.

## 10. Testing
- EditMode: hex math (world to hex and back, neighbors, blocked and occupied), unlock and slot tables per level
  cleared, squad validation, profile migration.
- PlayMode: deploy on valid and invalid hexes, select and move, reservation of destinations, each unit ability
  (draw fire, mend, revive, long range, seize, wrecker), defeat rule, reinforcement redeploy, ladder progression
  from a fresh profile, the squad builder.
- The playthrough bot is rewritten to deploy and advance units, and is the tool for balancing all ten levels.
- **Balance target (call):** with zero research, no mercenary, and a sensible squad built from what is unlocked at
  that level, the bot wins every level, and its surviving soldier count trends down from Level 1 to Level 10. The
  bot is the gate for the tests; the owner's playtest at checkpoint 3 is the final judge of feel.
- Existing defense-battle and meta tests must still pass. Tests of deleted player stroke input are removed.
